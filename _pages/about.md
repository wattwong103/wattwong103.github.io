Watcharapong Wongkaew is a master's student in Civil Engineering — Geospatial Engineering at The University of Tokyo, working in Sekimoto Lab (IIS, CSIS) on the Generative Urban Foundation Model (GUFM): a decoder-only transformer that generates realistic human-mobility trajectories across cities from real data. His work sits at the intersection of geography, transport modelling, and machine learning.

He graduated with first-class honours in Civil Engineering from Chulalongkorn University and spent three years as a research associate at the Chulalongkorn University Transportation Institute. He also worked with the ARIISE Research Unit on railway-track management, infrastructure resilience, and climate-change adaptation, and spent a year designing data-driven public-transport policy at the Academic Division of the Pheu Thai Party, including analysis of the 2022 Household Travel Survey.

He continues as a lecturer and faculty advisor at Mahidol Wittayanusorn School, where he developed the National Geography Olympiad curriculum and trains students in geography fieldwork and human geography.

He also writes open-source tools around the research: [trajectory-viz]({{ '/projects/12_project/' | relative_url }}), a map dashboard for agent-based mobility output; [BKK/FLOW]({{ '/projects/13_project/' | relative_url }}), an open-data flood-exposure and evacuation platform for Bangkok; a 3D [atlas of Shibuya Station]({{ '/projects/14_project/' | relative_url }}); and [Eki-dle]({{ '/projects/15_project/' | relative_url }}), a daily Japanese railway-station puzzle. The rest is on the [Projects]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}) page and [GitHub](https://github.com/wattwong103).

The academic one-pager — papers, talks, and awards — is at [wattwong103.github.io/research](https://wattwong103.github.io/research/).
